01 — CORE SOLUTION

It began with a manual counting bottleneck in the Seogwipo livestock field

Working on silage subsidy support, sample analysis and pasture counting at the Seogwipo Livestock Division, we lived with manual inventories that took about 30 minutes per case — double counting, omissions and a separate paperwork trail. SiloVisionX replaced that bottleneck with one drone or smartphone photo and AI object recognition.

  1. 01

    Counted by eye, written by hand

    Bale silage was counted by hand, recorded on paper and retyped into documents. About 30 minutes per case, with the risk of double counting and omissions.

  2. 02

    Automatic detection and counting

    YOLOv8m-seg detects bales in captured imagery and aggregates the count. Confidence thresholds are tuned on site.

  3. 03

    One-click evidence report

    A PDF report with location and timestamp metadata is generated automatically — evidence in 30 seconds from a single photo.

02 — ODA EXTENSION · MONGOLIA

The same vision engine reads
flocks and pastures

An object detection and counting engine validated in Korea extends simply by changing the target. A drone surveys the wide Mongolian steppe and shows (1) herd counts, counted automatically and (2) which zones are overgrazed and where forage remains, at a glance.

①

Automated herd counting

Livestock individuals are detected in aerial footage and counted by zone, using the same detection and counting pipeline as domestic silage counting.

②

Pasture condition mapping

Zone-level forage is compared to separate overgrazed areas from those where forage remains. Grazing pressure hotspots are visible directly on the map.

Turning data into decisions

  • Zone-level grazing history → rotational grazing plans
  • Early detection of overgrazing → pasture conservation and recovery priorities
  • Herd counts, area and vegetation indices recorded as metrics → ODA performance evidence

Why this suits ODA — (1) wide pastures that are hard to inspect on foot are surveyed by drone in a fraction of the time, (2) objective figures such as herd counts and zone-level vegetation can go straight into performance reporting, and (3) it runs in a smartphone browser, so field teams carry no extra hardware or training burden.
